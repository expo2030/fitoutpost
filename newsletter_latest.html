Editor's Brief

The hospitality sector dominates deal flow this week, signalling a decisive shift in capital allocation toward leisure and resort development. Egypt's £200m World Bank-backed expansion, the Mövenpick opening on the Croatian coast, and Rixos's Red Sea resort repositioning underscore investor confidence in leisure infrastructure across emerging markets. This pattern extends through the Caribbean and Australia, where hotel openings and experience centres are driving fit-out demand—a stark contrast to the prolonged office sector retrenchment.

Commercial activity clusters in the Gulf and Asia-Pacific regions. Havelock One's contract award for The Avenues in Khobar signals confidence in Saudi mega-projects, whilst Qatar's innovation showcases and tourism awards framework suggest sustained regional investment momentum. Australian infrastructure approvals, including Brisbane Airport's expansion, create downstream pipeline opportunities for specifiers, though the broader commercial development picture remains cautiously incremental outside hospitality verticals.

Procurement remains thin: just six active tenders this week, with the standout being Donegal's museum exhibition fit-out. The awards cycle itself generates visibility—FKM and NOS joining the Fit Out Awards 2026 sponsorship roster—but these signals reflect industry consolidation rather than new demand generation. The Irish market shows resilience through council-level Crown Square contract awards and McDonald's reinvestment.

The industry faces an uncomfortable question: can hospitality's cyclical momentum offset persistent commercial sector weakness? Specifiers should watch emerging markets' infrastructure timelines closely.
