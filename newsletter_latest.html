Editor's Brief

Hospitality remains the undisputed engine of fit-out demand, with activity sprawling across three continents. The Middle East and North Africa are experiencing sustained momentum: Accor's commitment to 25,000 new rooms across Egypt over three years, coupled with VA Group's Muttrah waterfront development in Oman, signals regional confidence in leisure and mixed-use expansion. Alongside this, Asia-Pacific hotels—from Curio properties in China to Grand Hyatt's Cayman Islands debut—suggest luxury brands are hedging geographically as legacy markets consolidate. Europe remains active but fragmented, anchored by hospitality refurbishments rather than greenfield scale.

Capital allocation reflects this geography. Egypt's tourism recovery ($8bn in first-half revenues) has triggered significant hotel investment, while China's family-focused openings (Peppa Pig's 2027 hotel) indicate operators targeting affluent domestic leisure. Ireland's defence and utilities tenders—including ESB fitout works—inject non-hospitality counterweight, though pipeline depth suggests these remain tertiary to hospitality's dominance.

The tender landscape remains thin. Irish Defence Forces vehicle conversion work and the ESB fitout contract represent infrastructure spending; a UN procurement for NIC interior renovation adds institutional weight. Notably absent: major contract awards. This gap warrants attention—are procurement cycles lengthening, or are suppliers securing work through framework agreements outside traditional tender visibility?

As brands race to deploy capital across emerging leisure markets, fit-out specialists face a critical question: can supply chains scale sufficiently to service simultaneous multi-geography hotel launches without margin compression?
